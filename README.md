# Archaeology

Archaeology studies past human societies through the material traces they left behind. It combines fieldwork, laboratory analysis, and historical interpretation to reconstruct how people lived, organized communities, and adapted to their environments.

## Core methodologies

- **Survey:** identifying sites and landscape patterns through field walking, mapping, aerial imagery, and remote sensing
- **Excavation:** carefully recording soil layers, features, and artifacts to preserve context while uncovering evidence
- **Stratigraphy:** interpreting the order of deposits so researchers can understand how a site developed over time
- **Dating techniques:** using relative dating and scientific methods such as radiocarbon dating to place finds in time
- **Material analysis:** studying ceramics, stone tools, metals, bones, pollen, and residues to understand technology, diet, trade, and daily life

## Common techniques

Archaeologists often use geographic information systems (GIS), ground-penetrating radar, photogrammetry, flotation, and microscopic analysis to document sites and recover evidence that would otherwise be missed.

## Why it matters

Archaeology helps explain cultural change, migration, belief systems, and human responses to environmental pressures while supporting preservation of cultural heritage.
